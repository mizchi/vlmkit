# anim-ir v23: scenes in Markdown, mermaid in — and what an imported graph asks of the layout (2026-09-09)

## Question

Item eight on the user's list was embedding: a scene where the prose is, and
the diagrams a repository already has. Two things were added — a remark plugin
that turns a ```` ```vlm-anim ```` fence into the runtime or a still, and
`vlmkit-anim import mermaid`, which reads the common subset of `flowchart` /
`graph`, `sequenceDiagram` and `stateDiagram-v2` into scenes — and the round
asks the usual question of the second: does a writer get a real diagram from
the guide alone, starting from the import, and what does the import leave on
the writer's desk.

The answer split in two. The sequence diagram imported and matched its fact
sheet on the first `check`, for both writers, with no edit. The graph — a
22-node pipeline with seven subgraphs, three of them nested under one — was
faithful (both writers counted nodes, edges and groups against the source and
found nothing wrong) and laid out badly: the canvas the compiler chose was
2989px wide, and every attempt to bring it under 2000px either changed nothing
or made two containers cross. Every one of those was a compiler defect, and
the round fixed four.

## What was added

- **`@mizchi/vlmkit-anim/remark`** — `remarkVlmAnim()`, a dependency-free
  mdast walker: every `code` node with lang `vlm-anim` becomes an `html`
  node holding `<vlm-anim>` with its timeline inline (the runtime once per
  document), a fence with `still` in its meta becomes the figure as inline
  SVG, and a scene that does not compile becomes a visible `<pre>` with the
  check's lines rather than a build failure. `renderFence(code, options)` /
  `parseFenceMeta(meta)` are the same function for markdown-it; the guide
  carries the VitePress recipe.
- **`vlmkit-anim import mermaid <file.mmd | page.md> [--as diagram|flowchart|modules] [--nth N] [--title …] [--out scene.json]`**
  — `flowchart` / `graph` → `flowchart` when a decision `{}` node exists,
  else `diagram` with subgraphs as groups (nested ones keep their `parent`);
  `sequenceDiagram` → `sequence` (`participant` / `actor as`, `->>` calls,
  `-->>` returns, `-)` async, `loop` / `alt` / `else`, `Note over`; `opt` and
  `break` become a loop frame labelled `opt: …`, `par` / `critical` / `rect`
  are flattened); `stateDiagram-v2` → `state-machine` with `[*]` as initial
  and final, `state "L" as A` labels, composites flattened, and no trace. A
  summary line says what came through; a `dropped / changed:` list names
  what did not, one line each (`style` / `linkStyle` / `classDef` / `click`
  counted, a `direction` inside a subgraph, `opt` drawn as a loop, `par`
  flattened, RL / BT drawn as LR / TB); a `notes:` list names labels over
  300px wide, which is where an imported graph's width comes from.
- **A `diagram` without a `canvas` is sized to fit** — see below for how,
  because the first two estimates were the round's defects.
- **A container may hold only containers**: the validator no longer demands
  a node of its own from a group another group names as `parent` (a subgraph
  of subgraphs).

## What was wrong, and what was fixed

**1. The canvas estimate guessed from counts.** The first `autoCanvas`
(written this round, before the writers ran) took the widest label times the
widest layer, then per-layer sums of box widths, then a term per group —
2989px, 4175px and 6723px on the same 22 nodes depending on the formula, and
1444px once the group term counted only outermost groups, at which point two
containers crossed. Now the picture is laid out once on a unit square — the
layered layout places every node at a fraction of the free area that does
not depend on the size — and scaled until the tightest pair of neighbours
has room: two boxes in one layer need their halves and a gap, and over a
container edge both containers' paddings; two boxes in adjacent layers need
their halves and an arrow's length, and a label band when a container edge
runs between them. The estimate is now what the layout needs, not a guess
at it: the imported pipeline is **1751×1770**, under the warning, and
`layout` clean.

**2. A parent's children shared one band.** This is what pa measured. The
layout bands by outermost group, and kept an inner group's members together
inside a cell — so the three tracks under `Parallel` were one full-width row
with their members spread evenly per layer, and the visual track's 435px
"Visual Semantic Diff" box, centred, reached into both neighbours' containers
until the canvas was about 2700px wide. Now the group tree is passed down and
a parent's range is shared out among its children the way the picture is
among the roots: a child that owns its layers within the parent is a row of
the parent, children that share a layer are bands of it, each as wide as its
fullest layer. The v20 nested fixture (a `backend` holding `services` and
`core` beside `infrastructure`) is unchanged and still clean; the pipeline's
three tracks each got their own slots. The `cluster` option that ordered
members inside a cell is gone — the tree supersedes it.

**3. An arrow went through a box it was told to go round.** With the tracks
in their slots, one crossing remained: the a11y track's arrow into the merge
box ran through that same 435px node, 101px inside its text. `routeAround`
decides by the leg's dominant axis — a leg at 30° from above right to below
left is "mostly horizontal", so the detour goes over or under the box — and a
waypoint under the box is reached through the box. It now tries the other
pair of sides, then the corners nearest the leg, and takes the shortest
waypoint that clears. The corpus (98 scenes) is unchanged against the v21
baseline; the pipeline is clean.

**4. The import was silent about the two things that mattered.** pa asked
why `direction TB` inside a subgraph produced no `dropped` line ("silently
applied or an accounting gap?" — a gap); pb asked for a warning about label
widths up front ("the import could warn about multi-line labels or deeply
nested groups up front"). Both now: a `direction` inside a subgraph is
dropped and counted, and a `notes:` list names every label over 300px wide,
with the reason it matters — the canvas grows to fit the widest label in
each layer, and shortening any other changes nothing, which is exactly the
three rounds pa lost.

Guide: `kind: diagram` gains a `groups` row (pa found the field only through
`schema --kind diagram`: "a reader following only `docs/anim-ir.md` top to
bottom would reasonably conclude `groups` belongs to `modules` alone"), and
the import section says `--as` applies to flowchart / graph sources only
(pa "wasted one call guessing `--as sequence`").

After the fixes, both writers' pipelines as delivered: pa **0 ✗ 0 ⚠** (was
1 ⚠, 2472px → 1362px), pb 0 ✗ 0 ⚠ (unchanged), both `layout` clean; the
import as-is, with no edit: 0 ✗ 3 ⚠ — the three "it is a still" warnings
and nothing about the canvas.

## The ceiling, named

The brief's second graph — the repository's own 32-node, 47-edge project
structure, five subgraphs — was imported for the round and not given to a
writer. It lays out at **13277px wide**. The reason is now legible from the
unit layout: bands are as wide as their fullest layer *in boxes*, and the
`cli` band has one layer of nine commands, so nine of twenty-six slots go to
it; every slot is one width, and the widest pair in that layer — two 500px
labels — sets it for all twenty-six. Band widths by content rather than by
count is the next layout change, and the first case for item nine on the
list: `why` — the compiler saying which pair of boxes set the canvas, which
pa reconstructed from the SVG by hand.

pb's other asks stand as notes rather than changes: that `canvas` bounds are
soft ("the layout algorithm still expands the content beyond the canvas if
needed") — a named canvas is drawn as named, and the layout does not clip;
the check warns instead — and a guide example for `"highlight": ["a", "b"]`
in a `diagram` sequence, which pb guessed from the annotation anchors and
which worked.

## Verdict

Embedding is in: a scene in a fence is the animation on the page, and a
diagram the repository already has is a scene in one command, naming what
it dropped. The sequence import needed no hand at all. The graph import was
right and the picture was wrong, and the picture was wrong in the compiler
three times over — an estimate that guessed, a band that spread siblings
evenly, a detour that went through — each found because a writer kept
measuring after the tool stopped telling them anything. Fixed, the imported
pipeline is a legible 1751px figure from the source alone.

Next: item nine, `why`, starting from the pair of boxes that sets the canvas
and the band that sets the slot.
