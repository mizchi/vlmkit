# anim-ir v13: still figures — five writers draw module maps, and the geometry learns to see lines (2026-09-06)

## Question

The still-figure presets landed the day before: `kind: modules` (a module map
with dependency layers and containers), `groups` on `diagram`, and the `still`
verb. No writer had used them. This round asks the v9 question of a figure
rather than an animation: **can a fresh agent, from `docs/anim-ir.md` alone,
draw a module map or a dependency graph that is correct and legible**, and
what does the geometry (`vlmkit-anim layout`, v12) miss when it says the figure
is clean?

Four briefs, five writers, no prior attempt visible to any of them:

| agent | model | brief | what it tests |
|---|---|---|---|
| fa | sonnet | `modules-this-workspace` | a real codebase from its `package.json` files; the `repo` generator forbidden |
| fb | sonnet | `modules-ports-adapters` | a rule (every dependency points inward) plus one dependency that must be shown as forbidden |
| fc | sonnet | `depgraph-import-cycle` | nine modules, one cycle to make visible and name the cut |
| fd | sonnet | `modules-request-walk` | one file as both the onboarding map and a walked request; two async hops to mark |
| fe | haiku | `modules-ports-adapters` | the smaller model on fb's brief |

Success adds two things to the usual `check` exit 0: `layout` reports no issue,
and an edge-fidelity hand count (every listed dependency drawn, none invented).

Zero hand-typed coordinates, colours or canvas sizes in five scenes; every
dependency drawn and none invented; `check` green and `layout` clean for all
five. And every one of the five stills had a line through a label:

> fe's "Core domain" container label with the HTTP → services arrow drawn
> straight across it; fd's "platform" label under two edges and a callout
> with two dependencies running through its text; fa's "pure (no Playwright)"
> label with 56px of edge inside it and the arrow from the root CLI vanishing
> behind `mcp`; fc's `router`, `middleware`, `validation` and `repo` with edges
> passing behind them; fb's "domain (owns the port)" under two arrows.

The v12 geometry reads text-on-text, text-under-box and text-past-edge. None
of these is any of those. This is the round's finding: **the check the writers
trusted could not see the defect every one of them produced**, and the
compiler produced it because a layered layout with straight edges and
containers has three places for a line to go wrong — through a label, behind
a box that is not its end, under an annotation — that nothing measured.

## The friction, verbatim, and what each became

**fa — the layer rule was not the one in the prose.**

> "The guide says a module's layer is 'one below the deepest module it depends
> on' — read naturally, that's a bottom-up function of a node's own
> dependencies. It isn't. … two modules with identical dependency sets
> rendered on different layers once one was reachable from a third root
> module in 1 hop and the other only in 2."

True, and a bug: the modules preset sized its canvas from a bottom-up layering
and then handed the nodes to the shared layered layout, which walks from the
sources. `layoutNodes` now takes `layering: "sinks"`, which `modules` uses:
what a module depends on decides its layer, and what depends on it does not
move it. The prose now says so in those words.

**fb, fe — no way to say "this dependency must not exist".**

> fb: "The guide documents a `theme.bad` colour token by name, presumably for
> exactly 'this is wrong,' but no op anywhere lets an author apply it.
> `relate` always renders in accent (amber) … a weaker signal for 'delete
> this' than red would be." "That accent-colour behavior for `relate` is
> nowhere stated."

> fe: "The guide's `deps` section shows `style: 'arrow' | 'line'` but doesn't
> explain when to use each. … When I needed to mark a forbidden/bad
> dependency, I had to guess that `line` (without arrowhead) was the right
> choice."

fe's guess made the forbidden edge a real dependency: the layout pushed
Postgres below the domain and reported a cycle, and fe fixed the cycle by
deleting a true dependency (`repo-port → domain`) — a wrong picture with a
green check. Edges now take `"style": "forbidden"` (dashed, `bad` colour,
labelled ✗ unless labelled, ignored by the layout and the cycle check) and
`"dashed"` (fa's optional peer, still laid out); `relate` takes `"tone":
"bad" | "muted"`, and the guide states the default colour.

**fc, fd — `highlight` would not take an edge, and the message blamed a typo.**

> fd: "`unknown node "payments->queue" → did you mean "payments"?` reads like
> a typo suggestion, not 'this op can't target edges at all.'"

> fc: "The field table says `sequence` anchors are 'module ids, group ids,
> `"a->b"`' without saying which op takes which shape — `highlight` rejects
> `"a->b"`, `callout` accepts it silently. Found the boundary only by trial."

`highlight` / `unhighlight` now take an edge: the stroke and its label take
the accent. The sequence row of the guide says which op takes what.

**fc — the same hint every round, and a cycle laid out "nonsensically".**

> "`check`'s overlap/overflow hint is the same generic sentence every time —
> 'try another `side` or a shorter label' — never 'try a different anchor,'
> even though switching the callout's anchor (edge → node) is what actually
> fixed the collision after 3 rounds."

> "Nothing in the guide warns that a cycle warning also means individual
> cyclic nodes get placed nonsensically by the automatic layering — `events`
> renders in the very top row, level with `server`."

The hint now offers the anchor as a lever. The cycle: under the sinks
layering the cut edge is drawn against the flow (upward), which is what a
cycle looks like; the warning names the edge the layout cut and offers
`forbidden` as the way to mark the one to remove.

## What the geometry learned: `crossed`

`vlmkit-anim layout` reports a fourth defect: **a line through a text, or
through a box that is not one of its ends**, measured as the pixels of
stroke inside the box (straight lines, bent edges and sampled arcs alike; a
faded or still-drawing stroke is not there yet). Exempt: a label's own edge, a
callout's own pointer, siblings of one annotation, a haloed label (below), and
a callout pointer over a labelled box — a thin line through a neighbouring
cell is what a hand-drawn callout does to reach an interior one; over a
free-standing label it is reported.

Run on the five scenes and the two module scenes the repository already
carried, at the compiler the writers used:

| scene | crossings before | after |
|---|---|---|
| fa — the workspace | 26 | 0 |
| fb — ports and adapters | 13 | 0 |
| fc — the import cycle | 22 | 0 |
| fd — checkout walk (12 frames) | 24 | 2 |
| fe — ports and adapters (haiku) | 6 | 0 |
| `fixtures/modules-web-service.json` (the guide's own example, with its sequence) | 9 | 0 |
| `docs/diagrams/vlmkit-architecture` (the `repo` generator's output) | 38 | 0 |

fd's two are one callout on a dense edge with a neighbouring dependency 18px
through its text: every candidate spot was worse, and the least bad was
chosen deliberately (below). The seven other kinds' fixtures went 0 → 0
except where a label sat on a line by design (chart value labels on grid
lines, message labels on lifelines); those are now haloed rather than moved.

## What the compiler learned, to earn the check

Each from a defect in the table, none from imagination:

- **Layer order.** Members of a layer are ordered by the barycentre of their
  neighbours (three sweeps), so edges run as straight as they can.
- **Edge routing.** An edge that would pass behind a box that is not one of
  its ends bends around it: a waypoint level with the box, past the side the
  line already leans to, far enough out that neither leg clips a corner;
  passes repeat while a new leg finds a new box. A bent edge is a `path` with
  a head; `flow` follows its waypoints. The workspace map's arrows from the
  CLI to `heal` and `capture` no longer disappear behind `mcp` and `generate`.
- **Full-width containers.** A group whose layers hold nothing but its
  members is a row (or column) across the whole picture; only groups that
  share a layer with something else get bands. fd's map became the
  frontend / domain / platform stack the brief described instead of three
  staggered columns.
- **Container labels off edges.** Eight candidate corners (inside top, inside
  bottom with the container growing a band for it, just outside above and
  below), the first free one no edge runs through, else the least crossed.
- **Annotations off strokes.** Callouts, readouts and relation labels treat
  visible strokes as they treat text; a callout's pointer must not run
  through another box; a callout on an edge sits by its middle rather than
  beside the diagonal's whole span; a wide callout above or below slides
  sideways to stay on the canvas instead of being skipped. The placement is
  scored — a covered text is never acceptable, a crossed stroke or a blocked
  pointer costs its length — and the least bad spot is taken when none is
  clean, where an unchecked fallback once put fd's callout squarely on
  `payments`.
- **Arcs.** A relation's arc picks the side that clears everything, is judged
  by sampling the curve (a Bézier clears its apex's line only at the middle),
  and enters each box on the side facing its bulge rather than down through
  the space above it where a callout sits.
- **Halos.** Edge, message, relation and anchored-readout labels are drawn
  with a background-coloured outline, so a line under them breaks around the
  glyphs; the geometry does not count a crossing on a haloed label.

## What this round did not settle

- fd's remaining two crossings, and the arc in the modules fixture that had to
  be dodged by shortening the fixture's own callout text from "the only shared
  state" to "only shared state": when a node is hemmed in on three sides, a
  callout has nowhere clean to go, and the honest fix (route the relation to
  the far side of the node, or let the canvas grow on the left) is not built.
- A callout's pointer through a neighbouring matrix cell is accepted by the
  geometry and by the placement (it costs its length); the v11 vector-clock
  fixture carries six of them. Whether a reader minds is a v12-style question
  the contact-sheet readers have not been asked.
- Four v10 scenes (`db`, `dc`, `dd`, `de`) carry overlaps and clipped texts
  that predate this round and were never re-compiled after the v12 fixes; and
  the matrix re-edit scene shows a flying token resting on a cell at four
  frame boundaries. Neither is new; both are now visible in one command.
- No vision reader saw these stills. The v12 protocol (`review`) applies as
  is; the `crossed` kind is in its brief.
