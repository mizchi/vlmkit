# anim-ir v24: `why` — the compiler's account of the picture, and bands sized by content (2026-09-09)

## Question

v23 ended with a writer who found the pair of boxes that set a 2472px canvas
by dumping the SVG's rects, after three rounds spent shortening labels that
were never the widest. `check` had reported the consequence — the width —
and nothing about its cause. Item nine on the user's list was `why`: the
compiler saying what it decided and from what. This round adds it, uses it
to make the one layout change v23 named (bands as wide as their boxes, not
their count), and asks two writers to bring a 32-node graph from 8882px to
one screen with the account in hand — recording, per round, *what told them*
which lever to pull.

## What was added

- **`vlmkit-anim why scene.json [--about id] [--json]`.** For a `modules` or
  `diagram` scene, collected while compiling into `Timeline.meta.why` and
  printed in the order a reader looks:

  ```
  canvas — what set each axis
    width 1583: DEP_GRAPH (206px, in Track_Intent) sits 10% of the width from the edge and needs 141px of room there, so 1478px + 104px of margins
    height 1570: VIS_SEM (70px, in Track_Visual) and CROSS (53px, in Merge) need 142px between their centres (both boxes' halves, a gap, and their containers' paddings) and the layout put them 10% of the height apart, so 1416px + 154px of margins
  rows — containers that own their layers
    Input: a row of the picture — layer 1 holds nothing else
    Parallel: a row of the picture — layers 2–5 hold nothing else
  bands — containers side by side, and their share
    Track_Visual: a band across 40% inside Parallel — shares layers with Track_Intent, Track_A11y; its fullest layer is VIS_SEM (435px of boxes)
  layers — what put each box where it is along the flow
    CROSS: layer 6 of 10 — one past VIS_SEM (VIS_SEM → CROSS)
  detours — edges bent round a box
    edge-12 (A11Y_SEM → CROSS): bends past the bottom-right corner of VIS_SEM — the straight line ran 103px through its box
  ```

  The canvas line is the pair (or the box at the edge) whose room set the
  axis, the room it needed and the fraction of the axis the layout gave it:
  the two levers a writer has. Rows and bands come from the layout's own
  assignment; a layer line names the one edge that put a box there
  ("one past X" in a diagram, "one before X, which it depends on" in a
  module map); a detour line comes from the router. Annotations that did not
  land on the side they asked for are listed last. Kinds that lay out by
  their own rule say "nothing recorded".
- **`check` quotes the cause.** The canvas warning now ends with the pair
  that set the axis and, when containers sit side by side across it, their
  count and shares: "6 containers sit side by side across the picture (cli
  40%, api 10%, packages 26%, backends 13%, outputs 9%, the ungrouped 3%),
  each as wide as its fullest layer's boxes". A crossing names the edge by
  its ends (`edge-24: diff → capture`), not only its id. The "no sequence"
  warning on a `diagram` suggests `kind: modules` when the picture, not the
  motion, is the point.
- **Bands sized by content.** A container that shares a layer with another
  gets a share of the cross axis proportional to the sum of its fullest
  layer's boxes, and the boxes in a layer share the band in proportion. The
  32-node import went from 13277px to 8882px wide; the imported pipeline
  from 1751px to 1386px, and the 98-scene corpus was unchanged in `layout`.
- **One canvas estimator.** A module map's canvas was a count-based guess of
  its own; `modules` now hands sizing to the diagram compiler's pairwise
  estimate, keeping its old room as a floor (below it a relation's arc and
  two dependency arrows ran through labels on three corpus maps). The
  paddings two boxes keep across a container edge are now their outermost
  containers' that do not hold the other — siblings under one parent keep
  14px each, boxes in different roots keep the roots' — which is what the
  v20 nested fixture's 7% container crossing at the minimum canvas was.

## The writers

One brief, `why-figure`: the 32-node, 47-edge project-structure import,
8882×1037 with ten lines through labels, to be brought under 2000px on both
sides and `layout` clean without losing a node or an edge (a fact sheet
fixes both lists). Relabel, break lines, regroup, flatten, change `layout`
— anything but the graph. Five rounds; and per round, *what told you*.

| agent | model | first `check` | canvas per round | rounds to green | final | `layout` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| qa | sonnet | 0 ✗ 14 ⚠ | 8882×1037 → 4615×913 → 7031×992 → 2044×1031 → 2006×1149 → **1805×1149** | 5 (cap) | 0 ✗ 0 ⚠, `diagram`, no groups, wrapped labels, one-beat walk | clean |
| qb | haiku | 0 ✗ 14 ⚠ | 8882×1037 → 1302×2483 → 986×738 → **1806×757** | 3 | 0 ✗ 0 ⚠, `modules`, no groups, one-word labels | clean |

Both green, both by the same structural move — flattening the five
containers — and both name the same line as what told them:

> qb, round 2: "The `why` output from round 1 explained: 'bands — containers
> side by side, and their share: cli: a band across 40% of the picture —
> shares layers with api, packages, backends, outputs'. … By flattening all
> groups, I eliminated banding and made every layer a full-width row."

> qa, round 0: "All five groups share layers with each other … so every one
> of them becomes its own vertical band running the full height, side by
> side … With 5-6 bands each only a few percent of the width, the absolute
> pixel gap between two boxes in different bands (workflow/openapi, 249px)
> has to be blown up to fill that tiny percentage, which is what drives the
> canvas to 8882px."

That is the diagnosis pa reconstructed from rects in v23, read off one
command in v24 — by both models, on the first look. The rounds they spent
afterwards were on two other things, and both are now in the tool:

**The canvas warning named the pair, not the structure.** Both asked for
the same sentence:

> qa: "a single line in `check`'s own canvas warning — something like '5
> groups share layers with each other, contributing Npx' the way it already
> names the two widest boxes — would have pointed at the actual lever
> immediately instead of after a `why` read and a cross-reference."

> qb: "The warning could say: 'the five groups share layers, creating 5
> bands side by side; each is as wide as its fullest layer.'"

`check` says it now (above), from the band entries `why` already had.

**Switching `diagram` to `modules` changed the picture.** Both switched
kinds to lose the "no sequence" warning; for qa it made the canvas worse
(4615 → 7031) and cost the round, for qb it changed the canvas after the
picture was already under budget (986 → 1806):

> qa: "the guide … doesn't say that switching kind also changes the
> *layering algorithm* (diagram layers 'one past' the thing that points at a
> node; modules layers 'one below the deepest thing it depends on'), which
> can pack more or fewer nodes into the widest layer."

> qb: "After changing to `modules` … the canvas reported 1806×757 — almost
> doubled in width. … I had no way to predict it without running `check`
> again."

Two causes. The layering rule is by design (a module map layers from its
leaves so that two modules with the same dependencies share a layer) and is
now in the guide, with qa's numbers; the `layers` lines of `why` say which
rule placed each box. The canvas jump was not by design: `modules` had a
count-based estimate of its own, and it is gone — one estimator for both
kinds, with the module map's old room kept as a floor.

Smaller asks, also done: the crossing warning names the edge's ends (qa:
"names an edge only by opaque id (`edge-18`), not its endpoints, even
though `why`'s detours section already prints edges as (from → to)"); the
"no sequence" hint offers `kind: modules` (qb: "the choice between
`diagram` and `modules` should be suggested when a diagram check warns
twice about missing animation"); the guide says the two axes grow for
different reasons and `layout` only swaps them (qb's round 1: `lr` turned
8882×1037 into 1302×2483 — "the height exploded").

## What the numbers say

| scene | v23 | v24 (bands by content) | v24 final (pads by container chain, one estimator) |
|---|---|---|---|
| pipeline import as-is | 1751×1770, clean | 1386×1770, clean | 1583×1570, clean |
| pa's pipeline (v23 writer) | 1362×1686, clean | 1254×1686 | 1318×1499, clean |
| project-structure import | 13277×1037, 18 crossed | 8882×1037, 20 crossed | 8882×1037, 20 crossed |
| corpus, 98 scenes | baseline | unchanged | unchanged (+ qa, qb clean) |

The 8882px figure is the honest width of five containers side by side whose
fullest layers hold 2723 + 680 + 1750 + 854 + 636 px of boxes; `why` names
the layer of nine CLI commands as the band that sets it, and both writers
took that band apart. The pipeline grew back a little at the end because
two boxes in different root containers now keep the roots' paddings apart,
not the innermost groups'; it stays under the warning and clean.

## Verdict

`why` closes the gap v23 named: the pair of boxes that set a canvas, which
one writer dug out of the SVG, is the first line of one command, and both
writers this round read the structural cause from it before their first
edit. What they asked for next — the cause in the warning itself, the
kind's layering rule in the guide, an edge by its ends — was cheap because
the account already existed; the only change of substance their rounds
forced was the module map's own canvas guess, which `why` exposed by
printing a different size for the same boxes.

Next on the list: `why` for the other laid-out kinds (state-machine, graph,
flowchart use the same router and layering and record nothing yet), and a
`why` for annotation placement beyond the asked-versus-landed note.
