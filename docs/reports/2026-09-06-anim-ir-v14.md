# anim-ir v14: the figure against its facts — does `check --expect` catch a green, wrong picture, and does a writer clear it? (2026-09-06)

## Question

v13 ended with two pictures that passed every check and were wrong. fe drew the
forbidden dependency as a real one (`"style": "line"`, the only style the
guide then offered), the layout obeyed it and reported a cycle, and fe fixed the
cycle by deleting a true dependency. gc walked the checkout request cleanly and,
asked to point out the two asynchronous hops (`payments → queue`, `orders →
queue`), highlighted `checkout → payments` and `checkout → orders`. `check` was
green both times; `layout` was clean both times. Neither reads the brief.

v14 gives the checker the brief. Each still-figure brief now ships a **fact
sheet** — `fixtures/anim-scenario/briefs/facts/<brief>.expect.json`, format
`vlmkit-anim/expect@1`: `modules`, `deps` as `"a->b"`, `forbidden`,
`highlighted`, `groups` — and `vlmkit-anim check scene.json --expect
facts.json` compares the scene and its final frame with it, both ways: every
fact drawn (in that direction, in that style, lit at the end, with exactly
those members), and nothing drawn that the facts do not have.

Two questions:

1. **Retroactively**: does the check name v13's two errors, and nothing else,
   on the scenes as they were committed?
2. **Prospectively**: four fresh writers — three of them the smaller model
   that produced both v13 errors — on the same four briefs with the sheets.
   What does the sheet say on the first attempt, how many rounds do its lines
   take to clear, and does a writer ever argue with it?

## The check, retroactively

| v13 scene | sheet | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| gc (request walk) | `modules-request-walk` | **3 ✗, exactly the error**: `"orders->queue" is not highlighted in the final frame`; `"checkout->payments" is highlighted … but the facts do not point at it`; the same for `"checkout->orders"` |
| gb (import cycle) | `depgraph-import-cycle` | 0 ✗ — 11 modules, 17 imports, the three cycle edges lit: all as drawn |
| fe (ports and adapters) | `modules-ports-adapters` | `domain->postgres is drawn as a real dependency; the facts say it must not exist` — fe's first mistake, in one line — plus the id mismatches (fe wrote `app-svc`, `repo-port`, `in-memory`) |
| ga (ports and adapters) | `modules-ports-adapters` | `dependency port->domain is missing from the picture` — the dependency the brief implied ("an interface the domain owns") and neither ga nor the brief's success line had pinned; the sheet pins it |
| fd (request walk) | `modules-request-walk` | the two async hops `not highlighted in the final frame` — fd had pointed at them with callouts, which the sheet does not count |
| fc (import cycle) | `depgraph-import-cycle` | the three cycle edges not lit; a group `"cycle"` lit that the facts do not know — fc had drawn the cycle as a container |

The two v13 errors are caught in one line each. The check also disagrees with
three pictures v13 had accepted: ga's missing `port → domain`, and fd's and fc's
choice of marking (callouts, a container) where the sheet says `highlighted`.
That is the sheet turning a stylistic choice into a rule, and the briefs now
say "highlight" where they said "mark"; whether writers mind is one of the
things the round measures.

A first run against fe and fb produced twenty-two lines each, most of them the
same finding restated: every dependency naming a misspelt id reported once as
missing and once as invented. The check now reports an id mismatch once per id
and says in one line how many dependencies wait on it. fe's report went from 22
lines to 11, with the forbidden-as-real finding visible in it.

## The writers

Four fresh writers, the same four briefs, each brief now naming its fact sheet
and `check --expect` as the success criterion. Three are the smaller model.

| agent | model | brief | first `check --expect` | of which from the sheet | rounds | `layout` at the end | hand-typed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ha | haiku | `modules-request-walk` | 1 ✗ 0 ⚠ | **1** — `"web" is highlighted in the final frame but the facts do not point at it` | 2 | clean, 0 crossed (13 frames) | 0 |
| hb | haiku | `modules-ports-adapters` | 0 ✗ 0 ⚠ | 0 | 1 | clean, 0 crossed | 0 |
| hc | sonnet | `depgraph-import-cycle` | 1 ✗ 0 ⚠ | 0 — a schema error (two action keys in one step) stopped `check` before the facts were compared | 3 | clean, 0 crossed | 0 |
| hd | haiku | `modules-this-workspace` | 0 ✗ 2 ⚠ | 0 — both ⚠ were `crossed` group labels | 2 | clean, 0 crossed | 0 |

Four for four green with the sheet; four for four clean geometry; no hand-typed
value anywhere. The sheet spoke once, to the model that produced gc:

> ha: "the first step highlighted 'web' (the entry point), but it wasn't
> unhighlighted before the final frame, which should only have 'payments→queue'
> and 'orders→queue' lit. This was caught by the fact sheet alone — the formal
> checks would not detect that the wrong elements were highlighted at the end.
> … without it, I would have produced a figure that compiled cleanly but
> communicated the wrong message (showing the entry point as the important
> highlight instead of the asynchronous bottlenecks)."

That is gc's error class — a lit thing at the end that the brief did not ask
for — found on the first run and fixed in one round with `{"unhighlight":
"web", "ms": 0}`.

The other three writers' sheets were green on first contact, and they said why
that still mattered:

> hb: "The fact sheet ensured exact spelling and prevented guessing whether
> 'server' vs 'http', or 'repo' vs 'port' was correct."

> hc: "The facts told me nothing I hadn't already encoded from the brief's own
> import list … I would still not have shipped without running it: with 17
> hand-transcribed edges from prose, `--expect` is the only check that a digit
> or arrow direction wasn't flipped somewhere."

> hd: "Without `check --expect`, I could have misspelled module names or missed
> a dependency. The fact sheet prevented errors that the generic `check` alone
> would not have caught — it only validates the scene is well-formed, not that
> it says the right thing about the actual workspace."

Nobody argued with a sheet. hd read the facts against the `package.json` files
as the brief asked and found them consistent; hb copied the ids verbatim.

### Friction, and what it changed

**hc — one action per step, and the guide did not say so.**

> "nothing in `docs/anim-ir.md` states 'one action key per step' outright —
> the `array` example mixes `pointers` + `caption` in one op, which reads as
> license to mix a step verb with an annotation op too. The error message
> itself was clear and immediately actionable, though."

hc had written `{"highlight": [...], "callout": {...}}`. The `diagram` field
table said "one action per step"; the `modules` table and the annotations
section did not. Both now do, with the idiom hc arrived at on its own — the
callout as the next entry with `"ms": 0` — written out.

**hc — a callout wider than the picture.**

> "the callout was clipped and crossed six edges, fixed by shortening the text
> and setting `side: right`"

Reproduced: a 70-character callout on the back edge of an eleven-module graph
has no spot on a 620px canvas, so `placeBeside` fell through to its unchecked
fallback — across six edges and 18px past the right edge. Callouts now wrap at
about 55% of the canvas width (160–360px) unless the writer broke the lines
themselves; hc's original text lands as three lines below the graph with zero
clipped and zero crossed. The pointer still runs over the `events` box on its
way to the edge's midpoint, which the geometry tolerates for a labelled box
and a reader may not.

**hd — group labels crossed at every corner.**

> "Group labels 'Measurement & Capture' and 'Integration' had edges running
> through them (15px and 16px respectively). Shortened all group labels."

Reproduced: two one-module containers straight under the root, under its fan
of ten edges; every one of the eight label corners has a line through it, and
so do the six middle positions added this round (inside top and bottom,
outside above and below, beside the container). When no candidate is clear
the label now takes the least-crossed spot with a halo, as edge labels do, so
the edge breaks around the glyphs; hd's long labels measure zero crossed. That
is the same admission as v13's fd: a spot can be hemmed in on every side, and
the honest options are a halo, a leader line, or a wider container.

**hd — where does a fact sheet come from?**

> "The guide section … did not explain where the fact sheet came from. I had
> to infer from the brief that package.json files were the source."

The section's first paragraph does name the sources (a brief, the bundler's
import list, the `package.json` files) and its last names `repo` as the
writer of the workspace's own; the brief named the file. The complaint is
still fair as a reading: a writer expects the sheet to be *given*, and this
round it was, by the brief. What the tool can do is write one — `repo` now
does — and the guide says so.

### What the round did not settle

- The sheet makes "marked" mean "highlighted". fd's callouts and fc's
  container were legitimate readings of v13's briefs and fail v14's sheets.
  A sheet could accept a callout at an edge as "pointed out" — not built; the
  briefs say "highlight" instead.
- The sheet fixes ids. Every writer this round copied them, which is what a
  fact sheet is for, but a map drawn before the sheet existed (fe, fb) fails
  on spelling first and content second; the id lines come first and the
  dependency lines wait on them, so the second is not buried.
- hc's wrapped callout points through the `events` box. The pointer cost
  tolerates a labelled box (v13, so callouts can reach an interior matrix
  cell); on a module map that tolerance shows.
- `--expect` reads `modules` and `diagram`. A `graph` walked by an algorithm
  has the same edge list and no sheet yet.
