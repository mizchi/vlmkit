# anim-ir v11: re-editing annotated scenes — do `value` and `relate` survive a data change? (2026-09-05)

## Question

v10 left the format with six annotation ops over named anchors and one open
worry: an annotation is addressed by id and anchor, not by position, so it
*should* follow the thing it describes — but nobody had yet changed the data
under one. This round takes three annotated scenes (two written by v10's
agents, one package fixture), hands each to a fresh writer with a change
request that moves exactly the data the readouts and relations track, and
asks: is every dependent literal updated, and do the annotations still say
something true at the end? The relation op `relate`, built from da's v10
quote, gets its first use by someone who did not build it.

| agent | model | task | frozen scene | what has to follow |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ea | sonnet | `README-v11-distributed` | da's vector clocks (distributed, 8 `value` ops) | one more event upstream: A's vector, two message labels, B's and C's readouts and captions; the frozen readout must not move; both closing claims drawn with `relate` |
| eb | haiku | `README-v11-matrix` | dd's Batched Pandora's Box (matrix, `group` + `value`) | a cheaper box: a cell, the decision arithmetic, both readouts, the final score; the competing rewards related |
| ec | sonnet | `README-v11-relate` | the vector-clock fixture (matrix, `value` + `relate` + `group`) | a receive: two cells, the readout in place, `A ∥ C` retired for `C ≤ A` |
| ed | sonnet | ea's task again, after the fix below | same as ea | same as ea, `nodes` order forbidden as a lever |

## Result

| agent | first `check` | rounds | dependent literals updated | annotations true at the end | brief met |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ea | 0 ✗ **2 ⚠** | 3 | 7 of 7 (A sends, B computes, B forwards, C computes, two labels, one note) | yes — `≤` arrow visible, `∥` faded, frozen `[0,0,1]` intact | yes, by reordering `nodes` |
| eb | 0 ✗ 0 ⚠ | 1 | 7 of 7 (cost cell, E[gain], batch cost, reward, best, cost text, score) | yes — `best = 6`, `cost = 1.3`, arrow `kept 6 > 1/2` | yes |
| ec | 0 ✗ 0 ⚠ | 1 | 4 of 4 (two cells, `vA` in place, the relation replaced by id) | yes — row A `2 0 1`, `C ≤ A` visible, `A ∥ C` faded | yes |
| ed | 0 ✗ 0 ⚠ | 1 | 7 of 7 | yes — arc `≤` visible, arc `∥` faded, frozen intact | yes, `nodes` untouched |

Read back from the compiled final frame, not from the logs: every `value`
shows its post-change text, every frozen `snapshot` / frozen `value` shows its
pre-change text, and each scene ends with exactly one relation at opacity 1
and the retired one at opacity 0. **The annotations survived the data change
in all four runs, and every writer updated every literal that depended on
it** — the arithmetic in captions included, which `check` cannot read and the
change requests deliberately spread across several ops. The hand count is
the metric; it came out 25 of 25.

Two writers were green on the first `check` with nothing to say against the
format:

> eb: "All calculations and value updates, relate syntax and cell anchor
> notation, check and explain outputs matched expectations perfectly … No
> significant friction — the guide was sufficient."

> ec: "getting two `relate` ops to 'replace' each other by both omitting `id`
> … worked exactly as documented on the first try — 'the second replaces the
> first (that is how to retire `A ∥ C` for `C ≤ A`)' is literally the sentence
> that described my exact use case."

## The defect the round found, and its measurement

ea's scene is a distributed one: three node boxes in a row at the top, the
title above them, the message lanes below. A `relate` from A to C with B
between them took the "run beside the pair" route built for matrix rows and
sort bars — and beside a node row there is no room on either side. The line
and its label were placed at y = −16. `check` said so, and its hint said:

> `→ move it, enlarge the canvas, or fade it out before it leaves`

> ea: "'Move it' isn't available (annotations take no coordinates, by
> design, and the guide is explicit and proud of that). I tried 'enlarge the
> canvas' first because it's the only one of the three suggestions that's
> expressible in the scene format at all — and it did *nothing*: the node
> row's y-position is apparently a fixed constant independent of
> `canvas.height` … I only discovered this by rendering an SVG frame and
> diffing the literal `translate(...)` coordinate of `node-A` between a
> height-400 and a height-900 scratch copy."

> ea: "The actual fix (reordering `nodes` so the bystander isn't between the
> two `relate` anchors) is not documented as a lever at all … that was closer
> to luck than to something the guide taught me."

Both halves are the compiler's fault, and both are the design note's own
rules turned on it: *every hint must name a remedy that exists*, and *a
diagnostic the writer cannot act on is a compiler bug*. Fixed in this commit:

- When neither side has room for a level line, the relation is an **arc**
  edge to edge along the straight line, bulging on the side with more room
  by just enough to clear the bystanders it would have crossed, with an
  arrowhead for `arrow` style. ea's original `["A", "B", "C"]` compiles with
  no warnings.
- The off-canvas warning, when the node is an annotation's, names levers a
  writer has (`side`, fewer things between a related pair) and says a taller
  canvas will not move it.
- The guide's `relate` row names the three routes and says the writer never
  has to reorder anything for it.

Then the same task went to a fresh writer (ed) with the node order off
limits: **0 ✗ 0 ⚠ on the first check, one round** — the three-round detour
was the tool's, and it is gone.

## Layout the frames showed (eb), fixed the same day

eb's `check` was clean, and its final frame had three things wrong that only
a look at the picture finds: the title *"Batched Pandora's Box: one adaptive
run (k=2, T=1/5)"* had lost its first letters (centred over the kind's own
canvas, which the readout panel had made the left half of the picture); the
`group` label *Batch 2* sat on the column header *box3*; and a long decision
caption was clipped at both ends. Now a title wider than its half is
re-centred over the whole canvas or the canvas grows to hold it; a group
label takes the next free corner when text already occupies its top-left;
captions wrap to up to three lines. All three under tests. None of this was
a writer's problem — eb never saw it — which is the reason to keep looking
at frames and not only at `check`.

## Guide gaps closed from quotes

- **The default id.** ec: "The guide never says what the *default* `id` is for
  `callout` / `group` / `relate` when you omit it … This is a *guess I got
  lucky on*." It is `"main"`, and the guide now says so, with the consequence
  that matters: two `relate` ops without ids are the same relation and the
  second replaces the first.
- **What "gone" means.** ec: "Whether the old, now-invisible `A ∥ C` relate
  would actually satisfy 'no ∥ line' was not something I could determine from
  the guide at all … If the README's grading were done by literal text search
  over the SVG … a naive check would have failed my scene even though it is
  visually correct." Replaced or nulled annotations fade out and stay in the
  markup at opacity 0; the guide says to read opacity, not presence.
- **A caption is not checked against the op.** ec caught the *fixture's own*
  lie: "B's existing `set` op is captioned 'B receives: max, then +1' but its
  cells never actually show a `+1` on B's own slot." The fixture now writes
  the second cell, and the matrix section says a caption is free text the
  checker does not read against the op's effect.
- **Explicit caption with `ms: 0`** (ed): the guide's only example relied on
  the generated caption; it now shows an explicit one and says joined means
  appended with ` · `, never replaced.
- **Where a distributed node anchor is** (ed): the node's box at the top of
  its lifeline, said in the anchors table.

## What the writers still wanted

- **Annotation liveness in `check`** (ed): "A `check --verbose` or a stats line
  like '3 annotation ops → 2 live at end' would have let me skip the manual
  SVG round-trip." Added to the stats line: `annotations: N ops, M on screen
  at the end`. It is the number every re-edit in this round verified by hand.
- **The re-edit READMEs** could be more explicit about what "in the existing
  style" includes (ea: does the preceding `note` count?) and that "keep the
  `mark`" means keep it *and* add the relation after it (eb). Those are the
  tasks' wording, not the format's; noted for the next round's briefs.

Eleven rounds, forty-nine agents. The question this round asked has a clean
answer: annotations addressed by id and anchor survive a data change, and
writers update what depends on the change without being told where it is.
What the round found instead was the compiler placing things where the
writer could not follow — the first defects in ten rounds that a clean
`check` did not catch and a frame did. The next measurement should be
visual: a contact sheet per attempt, read by a vision model for overlap and
clipping, alongside `check`.
