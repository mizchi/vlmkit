# d2-diagram v2: the sheet in the writer's hands (2026-09-14)

## Question

v1 withheld the fact sheets and found that a writer can ship a green, well laid
out, factually wrong D2 diagram with nothing in the toolchain objecting
([v1](./2026-09-14-d2-diagram-v1.md)). Its answer was `d2-facts.mjs` plus six
corrections to the skill. v2 asks the v14-of-`anim-ir` question: **with the sheet
in hand and the corrected skill, does a fresh writer get it right the first
time — and what is left that a sheet cannot reach?**

Same three briefs, same model assignment, so `d` and `h` are the same brief on
the same model with and without the sheet. Each brief now names its sheet, says
the sheet wins over the picture *and over the brief*, and lists
`d2-facts --expect` exiting 0 as a done condition.

## Result

Scored with the sheet the writers themselves held.

| writer | brief | model | fact errors | columns | rounds | facts green from |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| e | order-events | default | **0** | 95 | 4 | round 2 (width only) |
| f | shop-schema | default | **1** (width 113 > 100) | 113 | 5 | never; see below |
| g | checkout-calls | default | **0** | 75 | 4 | **round 1** |
| h | order-events | haiku | **0** | 65 | **2** | round 2 |
| — | | | | | | |
| d (v1) | order-events | haiku | **14** | 94 | 5 | no sheet |

**The comparison the round exists for**: the same brief on the same model, with
the sheet and the corrected skill, goes from fourteen errors and four phantom
boxes to zero errors in two rounds, at 65 columns instead of 94. Every v2 writer
wrote its cross-container connections at the root with full paths — the shape v1
taught the skill to teach — and no attempt in v2 contains a duplicated name.

Correctness is no longer where the rounds go. Of the fifteen rounds v2 spent,
**one** was about a fact (e's first draft was already 32/32 on structure) and
the rest were width and legibility. Three writers said the same thing in
different words: the sheet is a regression guard, not a discovery tool, once you
have it.

> "Honest read: on this brief the fact check is a cheap regression guard, not a
> discovery tool — **loop step 6 found all three real defects**." (g)

## What the round found instead

### 1. The check can report a false green, and did

f's own words, and the most important finding of the round:

> "Round 3 — `left: 420` / `top: 520` pin on `products`. Facts went green:
> `✓ width 71 ≤ 100 columns`, `10 ok, 0 warning(s), 0 error(s)`, exit 0. **It is
> a false green.** `wc -l shop-schema.txt` = 15 lines … the ascii render dropped
> `shipments`, `orders` and `customers` entirely and `d2` still exited 0. The SVG
> was *bigger* than before (boxes out to x=1893), so the picture had got wider,
> not narrower — the 71 was a measurement of a fragment."

The cause is structural in the checker: it reads the boxes from the SVG and the
columns from the `.txt` and never cross-checked them, so a `top` / `left` pin
that puts shapes outside the ascii canvas produces a text file holding two of
five tables, a smaller width, and no complaint from anything. Reproduced
verbatim before fixing.

`d2-facts.mjs` now compares the two: every box drawn in the SVG must be findable
in the terminal render, and a render missing any of them is an error naming them
and saying the column count measures a fragment. Verified: the pinned file fails
with `3 of 5 boxes are missing (customers, orders, shipments)`; the seven real
attempts, the committed example and a CJK figure are unaffected. (The first
version of the check passed the truncated file too — a `sql_table`'s label list
holds every column, so looking for `id` found it in another table. It matches the
shape's own name now.)

This also **changes f's score**: the 71 was picked up by an earlier scoring pass
over a mid-round file. f's deliverable is round 2's full render at 113 columns,
correct in every fact and over its width budget by 13 columns.

### 2. The width budget can be unreachable, and the skill implied otherwise

f spent four rounds on five `sql_table`s and no documented lever reached 100
columns: `direction: down` 152 → 113, dropping the type column 127 (worse, and
exactly the non-monotone warning v1 added), one wrapping container 121 (worse —
its padding costs more than its direction saves), and only the unsafe pin went
lower. Two further gaps in the same place: `--tala-seeds` moved it 113 → 100 but
**the checker rendered without the seed**, so the gate could not be satisfied by
the lever that satisfied the brief, and nothing said a budget might not be
reachable at all.

Fixed: `d2-facts --seeds 4,5,6` passes the seed through to the render it
measures, and the skill now says what to do when no lever reaches the budget —
split by container, or keep the wider figure and say so in the prose, and never
buy the number with a pin you have not re-read.

### 3. The skill's own suggested spelling failed its own done condition

v1 added "say it in the label" for a sequence diagram's returns, with the example
`reserved (ret)`. g measured what that renders as:

> "`reserved (ret)` (14 chars) exactly filled the lane and drew **zero
> arrowheads**: `│reserved (ret)│` … Worse, `(async)`'s parentheses were
> overwritten by line characters: `│────order.placed──async─────▶│`. The skill
> hands the writer a spelling that fails its own done condition 4."

And which character the line eats is not stable: the same file lost the
parentheses on one round and the full stop on the next (`order─placed`). The
route row now asks for a bare marker of four characters or fewer with no
punctuation, at the start or the end, glossed in the prose above the figure —
which is what g arrived at after two rounds — and says a legend inside the
diagram is not an option because `shape: text` renders empty.

### 4. `_` is an error outside a container

h read the skill's "reach out of a container with `_.` or a full path", wrote it
at the root, and got `invalid underscore`; it concluded the escape was broken and
switched to full paths (which is the right answer, reached the wrong way). `_` is
one level and only means anything **inside** a container: from inside `cluster`,
a sibling container's shape is `_.outside.stripe` — the depth v1's writer got
wrong in the other direction, inventing a root-level `stripe`. All three cases
are one gap, and the skill now leads with the form that always works (full paths
at the root) and states the two ways `_` fails.

### 5. A label on a border does not move when you shorten it

v1's advice was "shorten the label or move the connection to the root". e tested
both. Its connection was already at the root, and lengthening `HTTPS` to
`charges card over HTTPS` left the label on the identical border row at the same
width. Placement is positional; the levers that move it are the ones that reflow
the picture. The line is corrected.

### 6. Two smaller-model writers skipped the PNG, and said so

d (v1) and h both wrote a version of "a PNG read would have answered this" while
the loop's step 5 says to render and look at it. The step now says to *read the
file* with the file-reading tool and names what only the PNG shows; the PNG is a
deliverable. The three writers that did read it each caught something with it —
a dashed event that read as a return (c), the key badges and which row every
arrow leaves (f), crowding (e).

## Also corrected

- The sheets as hand-written in v1 carried decorative keys — `regions`, `tables`,
  `primaryKeys` — that `--expect` silently ignored, so the three regions and the
  five tables were never machine-checked. They are `containers` and `boxes` now,
  with `exhaustive`, and what the checker genuinely cannot see moved into a
  `_byEye` block the brief still requires.
- Container membership matched on the id's last segment while `resolve()` had
  learned labels, so v1's `a` — which abbreviated `gateway` to `gw: API gateway`
  — was reported as "edge does not hold gateway". Membership resolves the same
  way now and says where a member actually landed.
- The sheet's own advice for counting key badges was wrong: `grep -c` counts
  lines and an SVG is one line, so it prints 1. Both writers of that brief used
  `grep -o … | wc -l` and got four.

## What is deferred

- **No arrowhead metric.** "Does every arrow have exactly one head" stayed g's
  eye's job across three renders. It lives in the text art, not the SVG, so it
  needs an ascii-art reader rather than a DOM query.
- **No fan-out assertion.** e could not check that "one event to two consumers,
  not a chain" is *visually* legible; the sheet can only say both edges exist.
  `vlmkit-anim`'s answer to this class is a vision reader scoring the figure back
  (`review --still`); D2 has no equivalent and v2 did not build one.
- **No CI gate.** `d2-facts.mjs` is exercised by this fixture, not by a test: it
  needs the `d2` binary, which CI does not install. A test that ships a rendered
  SVG and feeds it to the reader half would cover the parsing without the binary.
- **Label semantics.** Nothing measures whether `ret` reads as "return" to a
  human. Same answer as the fan-out: a reader, not a geometry check.

## Honest read

The sheet did what v14 did for `anim-ir`: it moved the failure mode. Four
writers, zero wrong pictures, one over-wide one, and the smaller model — the one
that shipped four phantom boxes in v1 — now finishes in two rounds with the
tightest figure of the four. What is left is the half a fact sheet structurally
cannot reach: whether the render is complete, whether it fits, and whether it
*reads*. Two of those three now have a check, and the third is the next round's
question.
