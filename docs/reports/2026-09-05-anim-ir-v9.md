# anim-ir v9: explaining a concept, introducing a tool, a paper from this week (2026-09-05)

## Question

Eight rounds asked "animate this data structure" and the format passed. This
round asks the other question: **can a writer explain something the format was
not designed around, and how free are they when introducing rather than
walking through?** Four briefs, none naming a kind, all allowing several
scenes. Every writer had to record three counts we had never measured: every
hand-written coordinate or colour, every fact that lived only in a caption
because nothing on screen could carry it, and everything they wanted to show
and could not. The last brief is a paper submitted three days before the
round — *Batched Pandora's Box*, arXiv 2609.04059 (2026-09-03) — chosen
because its story needs several things at once (boxes that open in groups, a
running best and a running cost, a setup fee, a fork on what was revealed),
and the hypothesis was that such an explanation is a **combination of generic
structures**, not a dedicated kind.

| agent | model | brief | what it probes |
|---|---|---|---|
| ca | sonnet | `concept-vector-clock` | a value that must stay readable next to its owner; a comparison of two earlier values at the end |
| cb | haiku | `concept-http2-multiplexing` | before / after, ideally side by side; completion times |
| cc | sonnet | `intro-vlmkit-anim` | a presentation, storyboarded by hand first; freedom compared with a slide |
| cd | sonnet | `arxiv-batched-pandora` | the new algorithm: batch, reveal, running tally, branch |
| ce | haiku | `arxiv-batched-pandora` | same brief, smaller model |

## Result

| agent | kinds (scenes) | first `check` | rounds | position literals | hex colours | facts only in captions | brief met |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ca | matrix + **vector** (2) | 0 ✗ 0 ⚠ | 1 | 11 (scene 2) | 4 | the concurrent / ordered verdict | yes |
| cb | distributed + **vector** (2) | **2 ✗** (`note` rejected) | 1 | 24 (scene 2) | 13 | the time axis, per-asset sizes | yes, not side by side |
| cc | diagram (1) | 0 ✗ 0 ⚠ | 1 | 0 | 0 | the JSON text, the hint, the pitch's "small" | yes |
| cd | matrix + state-machine (2) | 0 ✗ 0 ⚠, **`explain` wrong** | 2 | 0 | 0 | 1.9 vs 1.2, cost split, both scores, the 2× bound | yes |
| ce | **vector** (1) | 0 ✗ 0 ⚠ | 2 | 17 | 13 | seven, including the whole decision rule | yes |

Every brief was met and `check` stayed almost silent — which is the point.
The correctness axis the first eight rounds measured is not where this round
found anything. What it found is in the other three columns.

**The coordinate fallback rate is the expressiveness metric, and it is 3 of 8
scenes.** Every scene written in a semantic kind has zero hand-written
numbers and zero colours. Every `vector` scene has dozens (52 position
literals and 30 hex colours across the three). Three of five writers reached
for `vector`; two of them (ca, cb) did so for something with no spatial
content at all — ca's words: "the format's most generic kind, used here for
something as basic as 'put two already-known values side by side and label
them'". ce went to `vector` first and never left it, hand-placing three
boxes and a "state panel" at `x=450`.

**The hypothesis held.** Nobody asked for a Pandora kind. cd's log, written
before touching the tool: "no single kind carries the whole storyboard — it's
assembled from a table-that-fills-in and a branch-that-forks, with several
facts that fit neither and have to live in captions". The five asks below
are all generic structures, and three of them were asked for independently
by more than one writer.

## What the writers could not say

### A value that tracks a number, next to the thing it describes — asked for by ca, cd, ce

> ca: "there is no way to attach or update an arbitrary text/value label on a
> node over time. So `distributed` can show *that* a message was sent but not
> *what the receiver's vector became* legibly attached to that process."

> cd: "a small 'value/meter' node (a named number that can be `set` and
> optionally shows its computation, usable standalone or alongside any kind —
> not just inside `matrix`) would have let the running cost/best-so-far live
> next to the boxes instead of forcing a whole second table row".

> ce: "A value label that updates dynamically ('expected gain: 1.9') would
> let the 1.9 vs 1.2 calculation happen on screen instead of only in caption."

Three of five, three different briefs. cd worked around it with an extra
`matrix` row; ce with hand-placed `vector` text; ca could not work around it
for the comparison and wrote a second scene. This is the single most-asked
addition and it belongs to no kind.

### Two pictures at once — asked for by cb, and forced on ca and cd

> cb: "Visual simultaneity — they cannot see both protocols' timelines at
> once to directly compare asset positions. They must remember HTTP/1.1's
> timeline while watching HTTP/2."

Three writers produced two scenes because one kind could not hold the
story: ca (the history in a `matrix`, the closing comparison in `vector`),
cb (HTTP/1.1 in `distributed`, HTTP/2 in `vector`), cd (the run in a `matrix`,
the fork in a `state-machine`). None of them wanted two animations; each
wanted two panes.

### A frozen copy of an earlier value — asked for by ca

> "`matrix` … is mutable-in-place — it cannot hold two frozen snapshots on
> screen together, so the brief's closing beat (compare C's `[0,0,1]` from
> step 4 against A's `[1,0,0]`) can't happen inside scene-1, because by the
> end C's cell has moved to `[1,1,2]`."

A "snapshot / recall" op — pin a copy of a value at time T under a label so
it survives later mutation — removes ca's whole second scene.

### Showing rather than narrating: code, a callout, an icon, a zoom — asked for by cc

cc storyboarded ten beats by hand before opening the guide, then measured
which survived: the pipeline and the pitch line did, unchanged; four were
**dropped**, each for a missing primitive it named — a multi-line code block
("no code-block or multi-line text primitive in this kind"), a callout
pointing at one line ("no speech-bubble/annotation primitive distinct from a
node or an edge label, and no way to point at a *substring*"), a wordmark
("no image/icon embedding"), a camera pull-back ("no canvas-level pan/zoom").
Its summary of the freedom question is the most useful sentence of the
round:

> "for the *pipeline* half of the pitch — six named things and how they
> connect — `diagram` was close to as fast as describing it in English … But
> the moment the pitch wanted to *show* something (the JSON text itself, a
> pointer from a specific error to a specific line, the tool's own wordmark)
> rather than *narrate* it, I was back to writing a caption and hoping the
> words carried it, which is exactly the leverage a hand-drawn slide has and
> this format doesn't."

### A time axis and proportional occupancy — asked for by cb

> "Would want to show Conn1 and Conn2 as horizontal timelines with asset
> 'blocks' filling the timeline, making head-of-line blocking visually
> obvious (like a Gantt chart)."

`distributed` draws messages, not occupancy; `chart` draws series, not
spans. A Gantt is a third thing. Recorded; not the first thing to build.

### A bracket around a group — asked for by cd

The "batch bracket" grouping two boxes became per-row highlights: "not a
visual grouping of two boxes". A group outline is small and belongs with the
annotation layer.

## Defects the round found, fixed in this commit

Three findings were not about expressiveness but about the tool lying to
the writer, and the rule from v8 applies — every warning (and every silence)
must be about the scene.

- **`note` is documented as a convention "in every kind" and `distributed`
  rejected it** (cb, 2 ✗ on the first attempt: `messages[6].note: unknown
  key "note"`). Added: `{"note": "…", "at" | "after", "delay"}` in the message
  list is a captioned pause that every node waits for, timed like a message.
- **A state-machine transition's `note` was drawn on the edge and absent
  from the narration** (cd): "All the numbers I had put in `note` — the whole
  point of the scene — were invisible to `explain` … Two different channels
  carry different content and nothing in `check` cross-references them." The
  generated caption now appends the note (`on lock: closed → locked / beep`);
  an authored caption still replaces the line.
- **A 4266px-wide canvas drew no warning** (cd: "legible arrow by arrow, but
  not as one glance; no warning is raised because nothing is off-canvas or
  missing a caption, which is all `check` looks for"). `check` now warns above
  2000px on either side, says what the canvas shrinks to on a 1280px screen,
  and names the levers (`layout: tb / circle`, shorter labels, split).

Guide sentences, from quotes: `vector` adds no start / end step (ca); `render
--step N` is the instant the step begins, use `--at` inside a fade-in (ca);
a matrix cell written as a number prints as JavaScript prints it, write
`"3/5"` to keep the notation (cd); the two new facts above.

## What this decides

The next build is not a fifteenth kind. It is two generic layers, in this
order, each to be measured by re-running these same five briefs:

1. **An annotation layer available in every kind**: a `value` (a named
   number or text that can be `set` and reads back in `explain`), a
   `callout` (text with a pointer at a node / slot / cell / state), a
   `snapshot` (a frozen copy of a value under a label), a `group` outline,
   and a multi-line `text` block. ca, cd and ce's asks are all the first
   item; cc's dropped beats are the second and fifth; ca's second scene is
   the third; cd's bracket is the fourth.
2. **`compose`**: several scenes in panes with a shared step sequence, so
   cb's before / after and ca's history-plus-comparison are one animation.

Both add nothing a `vector` scene cannot already draw by hand. That is the
test they have to pass: the same five briefs, and the coordinate-fallback
count goes from 3 of 8 scenes to 0.
