# anim-ir v12: the frames measured two ways — layout geometry and vision readers on the contact sheet (2026-09-05)

## Question

v11 ended on the first defects in ten rounds that a clean `check` did not
catch and a frame did: a title clipped at the edge, a group label on a column
header, a relation label under a readout. Two ways to read a frame suggest
themselves, and this round builds both and compares them:

- **Geometry** (`vlmkit-anim layout`): at every step, from the compiled
  timeline and with the compiler's own box estimates, which visible texts sit
  on other texts or under a filled box that is not their own, and which run
  past the canvas edge.
- **Eyes** (`vlmkit-anim review`): the contact sheet plus a review brief that
  says what counts as a defect and what JSON to return, handed to a vision
  model or an agent, whose answer is scored frame by frame against the
  geometry — both flag / reader only / geometry only, recall and precision.

No VLM API key was available in the environment, so the readers were fresh
subagents (the same method every round has used for writers), each given one
folder with one sheet and one brief and forbidden everything else, including
which sheets were "before" and which "after".

The four v11 scenes, twice: compiled by the **previous** compiler (the v11

main) and by the compiler as fixed in this round. Eight blind folders,

The geometry's own false positives were removed before the readers ran: a
matrix token sitting on its source cell at the start of its flight, and a
state-machine's token dot on its state label (25% of a small box), which set
the default threshold at 30% of the smaller box and excludes filled boxes in
motion.

## What the geometry found, and what it changed

Every one of the 77 overlaps on the before sheets was an **annotation placed
exactly where it was asked, on top of something already there**: a callout
"above" cell (1,0) sits on the column headers; a group label at its top-left
corner sits on a header, or — after v11 moved it — on a cell; a readout
"below" a cell lands on a group label placed earlier; a relation's label at
the arc's apex sits on a readout under the node row. Annotations now take
the nearest free spot: the asked side, then the other sides, then one box
further out, then the panel (reserving its rows), then a taller canvas.
Relations measure bystanders per node rather than per composite anchor
(`col:box2` is two cells; only one is in the way), an arc's bulge iterates
until it clears everything it sweeps, and a level line that misses fitting
by a little grows the canvas rather than arcing. The caption band is sized
for a wrapped caption. Every fixture and every v11 attempt now reads **0**
layout issues, and `check` carries the same findings as warnings.

| G2 (640px tiles) | before | sonnet | 0 | 2 | 4 | 0 | 0 |

Three things the table says:

1. **On the dense sheets the larger model reads what the geometry reads,
   in its words.** A: "the orange 'copied from A' callout box sits directly
   on top of the column header row (the small gray 'a'/'b' labels above
   column a/b are covered by the box)"; E: "a small annotation label near
   row C's leftmost cell (appears to read 'ordered') is partly hidden behind
   the cell's box border" — the very label v11's fix had moved onto that
   cell. Every flagged frame, no clean frame wrongly flagged, on 24 frames.
2. **The sparse sheets are a tile-size question, not a model question.** C's
   four defects are a 12px label on a 12px label and a 12px label on a column
   header; at the default 400px tile (the canvas at ~60%) sonnet reported
   nothing and said why — "the colored side annotations … render at very
   small font size. I could not fully confirm at this resolution" — and at
   640px tiles it found all four: "the 'Batch 1' batch-label and the 'best so
   far: 0.5' readout are drawn at the same position under box1/box2, so the
   two text strings run together into an illegible smear". G's defects are a
   single glyph (`∥`, `≤`) on a readout, and stayed unseen at both sizes.
3. **The smaller model is not a reader.** Haiku returned zero issues on
   every sheet and wrote "All frames displayed cleanly with readable text,
   proper spacing between elements, no overlaps" over a sheet with 27.

## What only the eyes found

Two findings the geometry cannot make, both real:

- E, frames 4 and 9: "the highlighted cell in row A / column a renders as an
  orange-outlined letter 'O' rather than a clear digit '0'". The highlight
  ring around a `0` makes it a letter at sheet scale.
- G2, frames 4 and 6: "a dot on A's lifeline and a small arrowhead near B's
  lifeline with the label [2,0,0] between them, but no connecting line joins
  them — reads as an arrowhead pointing at nothing". A step frame samples the
  message at the start of its beat, when its stroke has dash 0 and only the
  marker is drawn. **Fixed**: the renderer draws an arrow's head only once
  its stroke is drawn.

## What this settles

Geometry is the gate: exact, free, every step, and it found and fixed 77
defects before a reader saw one. The sheet is the second opinion, worth
having for what geometry cannot see — legibility, an arrow that reads as
nothing — and reliable only at a tile size the labels survive: 640px for
these canvases, not the 400px default, which the sheet's own header comment
has warned about since the sheet existed. The scorer makes any reader
measurable against the same frames, so a real VLM (`review --model`) can be
put in the sonnet column by whoever has a key.
