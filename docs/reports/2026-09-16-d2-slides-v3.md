# d2-slides v3: a reader catches what four green gates passed (2026-09-16)

## Question

v2's report ended by naming this round and its falsifiable claim:

> "Both defects that reached a delivered deck were *visible on the slide* and
> invisible to every gate — which makes a vision reader on `slide-NN.svg` the
> next measurement worth adding, not another writer round."

That was an assertion. v3 tests it: **shown only pictures, does a reader catch
the defect every gate passed — and stay quiet on the same deck once it is
fixed?**

## Design

One deck, writer `f`'s from v2, rendered twice:

- **arm A** — the pre-fix builder (`git show e6e316d^`): the deck exactly as
  `f` delivered it. Three bullets end mid-sentence and their tails are hoisted
  above the list as loose paragraphs.
- **arm B** — the same `deck.md` with the current builder.

Four readers, two per arm and two model sizes, each shown one arm's PNGs and
**nothing else** — no HTML, no `copy.txt`, no `slides.json`, no repo. Neither
arm was labelled; no reader saw the other arm or another reading.

Scored by `deck-review.mjs`, new in this round, against arm B's `slides.json`
(the `--sheet` flag exists for exactly this: one ground truth, two renders).

## Result

| reading | model | arm | read | invented | fidelity | order | SPLIT named | issues reported |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `r1` | sonnet | A (as delivered) | 19/21 | 6 | **0.70** | 1.00 | 1 | 7 |
| `r2` | haiku | A (as delivered) | 19/21 | 6 | **0.70** | 1.00 | 1 | 2 |
| `r3` | sonnet | B (rebuilt) | 21/21 | 0 | **1.00** | 1.00 | 0 | 0 |
| `r4` | haiku | B (rebuilt) | 21/21 | 0 | **1.00** | 1.00 | 0 | 0 |

**Answered, and cleanly separated.** Both readers of the delivered deck scored
0.70 and had the split named; both readers of the rebuilt deck scored 1.00 with
nothing to report. The claim in v2's last paragraph holds.

`r1` did more than score badly — it diagnosed the builder from six pictures:

> "your bullet-truncation/line-wrap layout is dropping each bullet's overflow
> text and re-inserting it as a stray paragraph above the whole list instead of
> after the bullet it belongs to — it happens on every content slide (2 through
> 5) and gets worse as bullets get longer (3 stray fragments on slide 4). This
> looks like a systematic wrap/overflow bug, not occasional bad luck, and would
> be worth fixing at the template level rather than per-slide."

That is the correct mechanism, named without reading a line of source, for a
defect that `check integrity`, `check copy --manifest` and `check a11y contrast`
all passed.

## What the round changed

### 1. `slides.json` — a sheet a reader can be scored against

`copy.txt` cannot serve: it is a **set** the copy gate looks lines up in, and it
lists bullets before paragraphs while the page renders paragraphs first. Order
is the whole defect class here. So the build now also writes each slide's
heading, its text **in reading order**, its figures, and the words those figures
draw.

### 2. `deck-review.mjs` — shoot, read, score

Shooting goes through the deck's own `#/N` navigation, so the shot is the slide
a viewer links to. Scoring is dependency-free and deterministic, in the
vocabulary `vlmkit-anim review` uses for a still figure: read / missed /
invented / fidelity, plus an order score.

### 3. The scorer names the split, so the reader does not have to

`r2` reported the right slides with the wrong mechanism:

> "Three consecutive bullet points end abruptly without completing their
> sentences … Text appears cut off at the right edge."

Nothing is cut off — the bullets genuinely end mid-sentence and the tails are
elsewhere on the slide. Same pattern as writer `b` in v1: right observation,
wrong cause. Since a missed sheet line whose words are all present *across two
or more invented lines* is decidable, the tool says it:

```
⚠ SPLIT:    "Latency on POST /orders past two seconds is almost always the card processor, …"
  every word is on the slide, the sentence is not — read back as 3 blocks:
    · "Latency on POST /orders past two seconds is almost always the card"
    · "processor, and the dashboard shows it per-service."
```

That line comes out of `r2`'s reading, which never uses the word "split" —
pinned as a test, because the diagnosis must not depend on the reader's
vocabulary.

### 4. Figure labels are not inventions

`r3` transcribed every box label — as a reader should; they are words on the
slide — and scored **0.55** for it, because the sheet held no figure text.
`r4`, which skipped them, scored 1.00 on the same render. A metric that rewards
reading less of the slide is broken, so the sheet now carries each figure's
words and they are counted apart, which doubles as the only record of whether a
label was legible at slide size (`figures: 17 label(s) read of 17 drawn`).

## What did not work — my own metric, and my own eyes

- **The `order` score did not catch this defect.** The hoisted fragments still
  appeared in the sheet's relative order, so order stayed 1.00 on both arms
  while fidelity separated 0.70/1.00. Order is for a genuinely reordered slide
  (self-test: slide 2 read bottom-up → 0.68). Recorded in the skill rather than
  quietly tuned, because the fix would be to make order mean something it does
  not.
- **My first reading was wrong.** Looking at the example deck's slide 2, I
  thought a container label ran past its box's right border and noted a
  finding. Measured: the box is 454px wide and the label is centred in it — it
  nearly fills the box and does not leave it. Dropped. The reader being fallible
  cuts both ways, which is the reason step 8 says to look at the slide a reader
  names before believing the reason it gives.
- A paragraph I briefly took for a leaked speaker note was my own extraction
  sweeping in the `<aside>`. Notes are where they should be.

## Also worth having

`r3`, on the rebuilt deck, had nothing wrong to report and gave editorial
feedback instead:

> "slide 4 ends by promising 'that is what the 409 on the next slide means,' but
> slide 5 opens with a 402 bullet and only reaches 409 third — a reader
> following the forward reference hits two other status codes before the one
> that was flagged."

No gate will ever say that, and no fact sheet either. It is the other thing a
reader is for.

## Next round

The loop now has a reader, and the first thing to do with it is **not** another
round of the same shape. Two candidates, in order of what the evidence supports:

1. **Put step 8 in CI on the example deck.** The `d2-slides` workflow already
   shoots nothing; a reading cannot be automated without a model, but the
   *shooting* and the *scoring* can be, and a stored reading re-scored against a
   fresh build would catch the day the builder starts splitting bullets again.
   That is a gate, not a round — cheap, and it closes the loop this round opened.
2. **A reader on the figures alone**, at slide size, scored against each
   figure's fact sheet. v3 measured prose; `figures: k of n` is a hint and no
   more, and a 6pt label inside a 700×560 box is exactly the case neither
   `d2-facts` (which reads ids, not pixels) nor a prose reader covers.

What v3 does not support is a fifth writer round: writers have been green on
the first write since v2, and both defects this loop has found since came from
readers and from scoring, not from writers getting stuck.
