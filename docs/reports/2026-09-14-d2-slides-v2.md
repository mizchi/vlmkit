# d2-slides v2: the sheet catches a figure four green gates passed (2026-09-14)

## Question

v1's headline finding was an argument: two writers *said* the page gates cannot
see a wrong figure, but neither shipped one. v2 puts the fact sheets and the
`--forbid` list in the writers' hands and asks the falsifiable version:

**does a sheet catch a figure that cleared all four page gates?**

Writers `e` (tala-talk, two figure sheets) and `f` (the re-edit, one shared
sheet across every write-path figure plus a forbid list). Same briefs as v1,
same forbidden paths, prompts updated to name the new affordances.

## Result

| writer | rounds | page gates (r1) | sheets / forbid | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `e` | 2 | all four passed on round 1 | both sheets passed | green |
| `f` | 2 | all four passed on round 1 | **shared sheet FAILED on round 1** | green on round 2 |

**Yes — answered, by `f`.** Its migration-window figure passed `check integrity`
on both views, `check copy`, and `check a11y contrast`, and the shared sheet
still said:

> `✗ forbidden edge drawn: checkout->orders`

`f`'s own summary of what that means:

> "the four page gates never look inside an SVG's D2 semantics, so a figure that
> compiles, renders and reads fine as HTML can still draw the stale/forbidden
> topology."

Both writers also needed a second round, and **neither second round was spent
on a page gate** — one on a sheet, one on content the gates cannot see. That is
the shape the loop was after: the cheap checks stop being where the time goes.

## The round's own finding: a builder bug both writers had, one caught

`e` spent its second round on something no gate mentions. Its round-1 deck
passed all four gates, and then:

> "`sed -n '15,22p' built/copy.txt` showed my bullet text scrambled: `"...D2
> creates a new" / "The fix is always a full path from the root" / "shape, so
> the picture..."`. The builder does not reflow a soft-wrapped bullet (a normal
> Markdown style, 2-space continuation) back into one line … All four gates
> passed anyway, since every text fragment was still visible *somewhere* on the
> page, just reordered."

Cause, confirmed: a bullet's continuation line fell through to the paragraph
branch, and paragraphs render *before* the list — so the second half of a
sentence appeared above its own bullet, and one sentence became two manifest
lines in the wrong order.

Then the scoring pass found the same damage in **`f`'s delivered deck**, which
`f` never noticed and no gate reported:

```
checkout no longer writes Postgres directly — it calls ledger,        ← line 5
…
which is now the only writer to orders.                               ← line 7
```

Two of two v2 writers wrote wrapped bullets; one caught it by hand-grepping for
a string its brief required, the other shipped it. Wrapping a bullet at 80
columns is ordinary Markdown, so the builder is wrong, not the writers.

**Fixed in this commit:** bare lines continue the block above them (Markdown's
lazy continuation), consecutive `>` lines are one pull quote, and a blank line
ends the block.

That fix then broke `e`'s numbered list — `1. Write the text` had been four
paragraphs that *looked* like a list because the text carried the numbers, and
rejoining ran them into one sentence. Caught by rebuilding both writers' decks
against the fixed builder before believing the fix. **So ordered lists are now
a block of their own**, rendered as `<ol>` with the marker stripped. Both
writers' decks rebuild correctly, and the committed example needed regenerating
for the one CSS line (`pnpm deck:example`, re-gated CLEAN).

## The sheet's other half: a generous match nobody documented

`f` lost its second round not to a wrong figure but to a name:

> "my migration-window figure passed all four page gates on round 1 while its
> box `pg` — despite having a non-'orders' *id* — still resolved to the
> forbidden `checkout->orders` edge because `d2-facts.mjs`'s `resolve()`
> loose-matches sheet names against **labels by substring**, not just ids. The
> label read 'Postgres orders (authoritative until cutover)', and 'orders' as a
> substring was enough."

> "the round-2 loss came entirely from not reading `d2-facts.mjs`'s `resolve()`
> closely enough up front — its header comment says matching is 'against the
> last segment of a drawn id' but doesn't mention the label-substring fallback."

`f` then relabelled a box that was right, to satisfy a sheet that was
over-broad for that slide. Both halves of that are the tool's fault: the
fallback is deliberate (`gw: API gateway` holding a sheet's `gateway` is why it
exists) and it was undocumented, and a bare `✗ forbidden edge drawn` line
cannot distinguish "the figure draws a forbidden edge" from "the sheet and the
figure disagree about a name."

**Fixed in this commit:** every message that turns on a loose match names it.

```
✗ forbidden edge drawn: checkout->orders (orders matched no id — it is a
  substring of box pg's label "Postgres orders (authoritative until cutover)")
```

The exact-id path stays silent, or every line grows a clause. The four widening
steps are now written down in `d2-diagram`'s skill, `f`'s figure is frozen as
`fixtures/d2-scenario/v2/label-substring/`, and `tests/d2-facts.test.mjs` pins
the clause against that render. The fixture sheet's `_why` now says the
migration figure is out of its scope — the sheet, not the figure, was wrong.

## What worked — their own words

`e`, on the sentence that made it check content at all:

> "step 7's 'the four page gates do not cover it' (which is why I independently
> grepped for the required strings and caught the split-bullet bug)"

The v1 fix worked in an unintended way: it did not make `e` distrust its
figures (they were right) — it made `e` distrust the gates' coverage, and that
is what found the builder bug. `f` on the same step, used as intended.

Neither writer mentioned the contrast count or the `CLEAN`-vs-warn confusion,
both of which cost v1 writers time. `f` recorded the figure-internals warn as
what the skill now says it is: "NO DEFECTS, 2 WARN (documented TALA edge-label
warn — not a failure)."

## Still open

- **Where a new slide belongs is unstated**, said by both `d` (v1) and `f`:
  "the brief only says 'where it makes sense'". Both placed it identically and
  sensibly. Still not a tool problem — document order *is* the ordering.
- **`--allow` cannot exempt a subtree** (v1 finding 5). Unchanged, and still
  nobody has been blocked by it.
- **No reader has looked at a slide.** Every check in this loop is text or
  geometry; `anim-ir` v12 and v21 found that a second, unlike reader sees what
  the first is blind to. A vision reader on `slide-NN.svg` or on a screenshot
  of the deck is the measurement this loop does not have — and, on this round's
  evidence, the next one worth adding: the two defects that reached a delivered
  deck (`e`'s scrambled bullet, `f`'s split sentence) were both **visible on
  the slide** and invisible to every gate.
