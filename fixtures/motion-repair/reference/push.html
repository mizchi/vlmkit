The quiet return of the corner bookshop

For a decade the story was one of closures. Last year, for the first time since records began, more independent bookshops opened than closed, and most of them were small: one room, a till, a reading chair.

Their owners describe the same customers — people who want to be recommended something by a person who has read it. Margins are thin and rents are not, but the shops that survive have learned to host: readings, book clubs, a children's hour on Saturday mornings.

None of this will bring back the chains. It may not need to.
